Google Cloud actually has dedicated pipelines designed to do exactly
this without requiring you to manually babysit an API. Here is the
architecture you will use to chew through those Qualcomm NPU specs and
Unsloth repos.

## The Cloud Ingestion Engine

You have two primary options in Google Cloud to handle this, depending
on how messy the PDFs are:

### 1. Vertex AI Batch Predictions (The Bulk Processor)

If you just need to rip through standard text and extract structured
facts, you use **Vertex AI Batch Predictions** with a model like Gemini
2.5 Flash.

- **How it works:** You upload all your PDFs to a Google Cloud Storage
  > (GCS) bucket, write a single JSONL file containing your extraction
  > prompts, and submit it as a background job.

- **The Output:** Gemini processes the entire bucket asynchronously and
  > automatically deposits the extracted responses natively as .jsonl
  > files right back into a destination GCS bucket.

### 2. Document AI Layout Parser (The Technical Decoder)

Because you are dealing with Qualcomm and Unsloth documentation, you are
going to encounter dense tables, C++ code blocks, and architecture
diagrams. Standard PDF parsers destroy this formatting.

- **How it works:** Google's **Document AI** has a Gemini-powered Layout
  > Parser specifically built for complex RAG (Retrieval-Augmented
  > Generation) pipelines.

- **The Output:** It preserves the inherent hierarchy of the document
  > (keeping tables and headings linked) and can verbalize visual charts
  > before outputting the structured data.

## The Workflow to Your Device

Here is how the data actually flows from the cloud back to your Termux
setup:

1.  **The Upload:** You sync your raw documentation from your device to
    > the cloud: gsutil cp -r ~/local-docs gs://your-raw-docs-bucket/

2.  **The Prompt:** You instruct the cloud model to split its output:

    - *Instruction A:* "Extract all compilation flags, hardware limits,
      > and API parameters into structured key-value pairs" (This
      > becomes your **JSONL Almanac cache**).

    - *Instruction B:* "Summarize the architectural concepts and
      > workflow instructions" (This becomes your **Markdown Wiki
      > context**).

3.  **The Sync Down:** Once the batch job finishes, you pull the
    > processed files down to your Termux environment. Your heavy PDFs
    > stay in the cloud, and your Snapdragon 8 Elite only has to parse
    > the lightweight JSONL and MD files.
